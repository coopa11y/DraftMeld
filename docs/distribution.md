# Community and official distribution

DraftMeld is one open-source product with two delivery paths. The software does not reserve product features for paying users.

## Community distribution

The community path is available to everyone under the [GNU AGPL v3](../LICENSE):

- complete source code;
- the production Dockerfile and Docker Compose configuration;
- published multi-architecture container images;
- every drafting, import, recommendation, accessibility, and data-portability feature;
- community support through GitHub issues and discussions.

Users may inspect, modify, compile, and redistribute DraftMeld under the license. DraftMeld cannot and does not prevent third parties from compiling their own executables.

## Official supported builds

DraftMeld plans to offer signed Windows and Linux packages through a third-party fulfillment provider. These packages are paid convenience, not a separate edition. They are expected to include:

- a verified, one-step installation path;
- signed and checksummed packages produced by the DraftMeld maintainers;
- a clearly supported upgrade path;
- direct help with installation and packaging problems.

Official builds are not available until the first public release and a fulfillment provider is configured. The project website and release notes must not present a price, purchase control, or availability claim before that work is complete.

Any official package must include the corresponding source offer, license notices, and provenance required by the AGPL and the project's release policy.

## Contributor access

Meaningful contributions may earn access to official builds for the current fantasy-football season. Contribution is broader than code and may include:

- implementation and bug fixes;
- documentation and tutorials;
- translations;
- repeatable testing and high-quality defect reports;
- maintained ranking-source adapters;
- accessibility review and screen-reader testing;
- design and user-research work accepted into the project.

Maintainers decide eligibility based on impact, quality, and whether the work was accepted. A single trivial or automated change does not create an entitlement. Sustained maintainers may receive continuing access. This program is recognition for contribution, not employment or a promise of payment.

## Sustainability principles

1. The community product remains complete.
2. Revenue comes from trusted packaging, convenience, and support.
3. Public claims describe current availability honestly.
4. User and league data are never a monetization input.
5. Contributors receive clear credit and the same license protections as every user.
